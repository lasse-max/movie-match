## 3. The plan: 2.0 in three tracks

**Track A · Embeddings (finishes the matching engine).** Goal: the final shortlist draws from thousands of on-taste films instead of about 45, so a film the couple hadn't thought of can win.
- A curated catalog (about 10 to 30k titles, above the existing quality floor), each film embedded once from its TMDB overview and keywords (OpenAI `text-embedding-3-small`, the model the de-risk used).
- Each player's taste vector is the average of the films they swiped "This vibe" on. It is built from vectors we already stored, so **a game costs almost nothing extra** (only a swiped film missing from the catalog needs embedding, a fraction of a cent). Embedding the whole catalog once costs well under a dollar.
- The retrieved films feed the **existing** overlap and bridge picker. No maximin picker. Availability stays a deterministic TMDB check.
- **Eval-gated.** Step 1 measures the real variety multiple (after availability and franchise dedup; 5.29x was an upper bound). Pass bar: at least 2x for broad-service couples, and no regression on the 17/17 set.

**Track B · Picture Palace redesign.** Prototype and brief are done. Lasse takes the brief to Claude Design or Figma and brings back a final. Otto writes Arthur's build brief. Arthur restyles components only (as with Marquee), then Cato reviews.

**Track C · Launch readiness (small, but must land before any public push).**
- Rate limit plus a daily spend cap on the AI routes (F3).
- A light product event log: game started, round completed, match or bridge, watch tapped, played again (F2). This is the 2.0 gate.
- Saved services (F4).
- Share card plus share link, if D3 is yes.

**GTM (later, target 10K users).** It needs Track C live first, so we can see completion, match and replay rates and the real cost per game. Before any monetising, check two things: Vercel Hobby is for non-commercial use, and TMDB's free API is non-commercial.
